# Tesseract 5 wins the lightweight Persian OCR race

**Tesseract 5 with the stock `fas`+`eng` models is the lightest practical starting point for offline Persian and English screen-region OCR in a Windows 11 .NET 8 utility, but it is a provisional pick that must pass an empirical bake-off before it ships.** It is the only candidate with a genuine Persian-specific model, and that model is tiny (about **0.29 MB** download for `fas` in the MSYS2 package) and Apache-2.0 ([MSYS2](https://packages.msys2.org/packages/mingw-w64-ucrt-x86_64-tesseract-data-fas)). PaddleOCR and RapidOCR have no Persian model at all; they read Persian through a shared "arabic" recognizer scored at only 81.27% on its own multi-language benchmark ([PaddleOCR](https://www.paddleocr.ai/latest/en/version3.x/algorithm/PP-OCRv5/PP-OCRv5_multi_languages.html)). Windows.Media.Ocr is eliminated for Persian because a real Windows 11 OCR capability list contains Arabic but no fa-IR ([Text-Grab #200 mirror](https://github-users.yhajoo.workers.dev/TheJoeFin/Text-Grab/issues/200)). The weak spots of Tesseract are real, though: an open Persian-digits bug, no evidence on zero-width non-joiner (ZWNJ) handling, no published accuracy on small UI text, and a stale primary .NET wrapper. A purpose-built small Persian recognizer (ParisaOCR, about 12 MB) benchmarks much better on book text, but it ships as Kraken safetensors, not ONNX, so it is an upgrade path rather than a drop-in. Almost every quantitative claim about speed, RAM and screen-text accuracy in the notes is unverified, so the recommendation below ends with a test plan.

## Only Tesseract and one niche model are truly Persian

Tesseract's `fas` is an official LSTM model in the tessdata, tessdata_fast and tessdata_best repositories, readable by Tesseract 5 because 5.x uses the 4.0 model format ([tessdoc](https://www.github.com/tesseract-ocr/tessdoc/blob/main/Data-Files-in-different-versions.md)). The data is Apache-2.0 ([tessdata_fast mirror](https://github.com/tfmorris/tessdata_fast)). The `ara` model is separate, and using it for Persian would likely substitute پ چ ژ گ (an inference, not sourced).

PaddleOCR is different. Its official coverage is the `arabic_PP-OCRv5_mobile_rec` model, which lists Persian among nine languages (Arabic, Persian, Uyghur, Urdu, Pashto, Kurdish, Sindhi, Balochi, English); the older v3 arabic model scored **73.55%** ([model list](https://www.paddleocr.ai/latest/en/version3.x/model_list.html)). The newest PP-OCRv6 family has no Arabic or Persian model, and the PaddleOCR team says it would prioritize one only if the community supplies data and dictionaries ([HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/PP-OCRv6_medium_rec/discussions/2)). RapidOCR is the same weights converted to ONNX: its `arabic` language type (alias `ar`) maps to PP-OCRv4 and v5 ([DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/RapidAI/RapidOCR/8.1-language-support)), so its Persian quality equals Paddle's. Whether the shared dictionary contains پ چ ژ گ, the Persian digits ۰-۹ and ZWNJ could not be checked because the dictionary files were not fetchable; ZWNJ support is doubtful since Paddle dictionaries normally omit invisible characters (inference).

The only Persian-trained small model found is ParisaOCR: about 3M parameters, 12 MB, Apache-2.0, 182 classes including Persian and Latin letters, both digit sets and ZWNJ. It reports 0.39% character error (ignoring spaces) on 750 verified book lines versus 1.19% for Tesseract, and 77.9% versus 71.1% on the PersianML printed-page benchmark ([HF](https://huggingface.co/givia/ParisaOCR)). Those are the author's own numbers on book scans, the card says it is weaker below roughly 14 px text height, and it needs its own pipeline for line detection. The Bina model family and a "Triple Threat" benchmark exist, but the benchmark is self-reported by the Bina publisher and has an internal inconsistency in its first table ([benchmark](https://reza2kn-persianocr-triplethreat.hf.space/)); it ranks a 270K-labelled Tesseract Persian entry at 66.29 against 9.84 to 11.47 for the larger Bina models (lower is better), which I treat as directional only. Heavy general models such as Surya (686M parameters) are out of scope for a light utility.

## Tesseract's Persian accuracy is decent on print but unproven on screens

Published Tesseract Persian numbers all come from print-like material. A custom Tesseract model ("fas_print", not the stock `fas`) reached 1.67% character error and 7.3% word error on book lines but only 71.1% words found on a different printed benchmark, which shows strong domain sensitivity ([ParisaOCR card](https://huggingface.co/givia/ParisaOCR)). A synthetic Farsi study found Tesseract at **84.41%** overall versus 73.28% for EasyOCR, falling from 92.45% on plain backgrounds to 67.17% on textured ones ([CIVILICA](https://civilica.com/doc/2248676); abstract only, model version unstated). Nothing was found for small, ClearType-rendered UI text, so the key claim a screen utility needs is simply untested.

Digits are the sharpest known defect: a "Cannot show Persian numbers" issue against `fas` has been open since December 2020 with no visible resolution ([langdata #155](https://www.github.com/tesseract-ocr/langdata/issues/155)). Fine-tuning is no easy escape; one Arabic-digit fine-tune degraded performance drastically ([tessdata_best #53](https://www.github.com/tesseract-ocr/tessdata_best/issues/53)), and a from-scratch Farsi training attempt on Tesseract 5.2.0 ended at a high error rate ([Google Group](https://groups.google.com/g/tesseract-ocr/c/MzzHGwwzclk)). For PaddleOCR the notes add a cautionary, self-reported and Hebrew-only data point that CTC decoders can delete runs of Latin text or digits inside right-to-left lines (12.63% CTC versus 2.33% attention-head error), which makes mixed Persian-English lines a specific risk for the Paddle family ([discussion #14971](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/discussions/14971)). Tesseract is also an LSTM-CTC design, so the same risk may apply there (my inference).

Right-to-left handling differs in effort rather than outcome. PaddleOCR has no built-in right-to-left reading order, and the maintainer's workaround is to group detected boxes into lines by y position, sort each line right to left, and apply bidi only for display, since the model already emits logical order ([discussion #14971](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/discussions/14971)). A .NET integrator must write that grouping code. Tesseract's ordering of embedded English and digits was not sourced either way. For both, the safe rule is to store logical order and never reverse strings. A realistic post-processing pass (all general knowledge, unverified here) normalizes Arabic yeh and kaf to Persian forms, maps digit sets to the user's preference, strips tatweel, and restores ZWNJ in prefixes like می- and suffixes like ها.

## Footprint favors Tesseract, with speed and RAM still unmeasured

| Criterion | Tesseract 5 (`fas`+`eng`) | PaddleOCR / RapidOCR (arabic) | Windows.Media.Ocr |
|---|---|---|---|
| Persian model | Dedicated `fas` | Shared arabic model, no Persian-only | None found (Arabic pack only) |
| Model size | `fas` 0.29 MB; eng fast about 4 MB (unverified) | arabic rec 7.7 MiB (v5) / 8.6 MiB (v3), plus detector 2.3 to 4.7 MB; cls about 0.6 MB (unverified) | Zero bundled; pack installed from Windows Update |
| Runtime | Native Tesseract + Leptonica, roughly 10-20 MB (unverified); needs VC++ runtime | ONNX Runtime native roughly 10-15 MB plus SkiaSharp native roughly 10 MB (both unverified) | OS component |
| Speed | 0.3-2 s per small region (unverified estimate) | Rec 8-21 ms per line; end-to-end 0.2-3.3 s per image on a Xeon, not Persian-specific | Not researched |
| RAM | Not measured | Not measured | Not measured |
| Licenses | Apache-2.0 data and wrappers | Apache-2.0 code and models; weights belong to Baidu/PaddleOCR | OS-provided |
| .NET 8 route | `TesseractOCR` 5.5.2 or charlesw `Tesseract` 5.2.0 | `RapidOcrNet` 4.2.0, net8.0, Apache-2.0 | WinRT, `net8.0-windows10.0.19041.0` |

The Paddle figures come from official pages: v5 mobile detection is 4.7 MB at 57.77 ms CPU and the v3 arabic recognizer is 17.3 MB of storage at 8.44 ms per sample ([model list](https://www.paddleocr.ai/latest/en/version3.x/model_list.html)), while converted ONNX arabic recognizers are 7.7 and 8.6 MiB ([oar-ocr](https://docs.rs/crate/oar-ocr/0.8.1/source/docs/models.md)) and the v3 detector is 2.3 MB ([monkt/paddleocr-onnx](https://huggingface.co/monkt/paddleocr-onnx)). Note that v3 and v5 detectors and recognizers cannot be mixed, and that on PP-OCRv6 the medium model runs slower under ONNX Runtime (3.31 s per image) than under native Paddle (2.05 s) ([PP-OCRv6](https://www.paddleocr.ai/latest/en/version3.x/algorithm/PP-OCRv6/PP-OCRv6.html)); since v6 has no arabic model, those timings do not apply to Persian. Both engines are small in absolute terms; the real size driver in the final package is the .NET runtime, not the OCR models. Heavy .NET alternatives are poor fits: Sdcb.PaddleOCR needs the Paddle Inference runtime and PaddleOCRSharp's package alone is 68.89 MB with an unconfirmed license ([NuGet](https://packages.nuget.org/packages/PaddleOCRSharp/)).

On wrappers, the charlesw `Tesseract` NuGet is 5.2.0 from November 2022, targets .NET Standard 2.0, and has 219 open issues including a crash on multi-language initialization (#678) ([NuGet](https://www.nuget.org/packages/Tesseract); [issues](https://www.github.com/charlesw/tesseract/issues)). `TesseractOCR` 5.5.2 (March 2026) is more current, Apache-2.0, ships native `Tesseract54.dll` and a Leptonica DLL, and needs the VC++ runtimes ([NuGet](https://www.nuget.org/packages/TesseractOCR)). A no-wrapper fallback is shipping UB Mannheim's 64-bit `tesseract.exe` (5.5.3, July 2026) and spawning it per capture, at the cost of model load each call ([UB Mannheim](https://github.com/UB-Mannheim/tesseract/wiki)). `RapidOcrNet` 4.2.0 targets net8.0, depends on ONNX Runtime and SkiaSharp, and also supports PP-OCRv6 ([NuGet](https://feed.nuget.org/packages/RapidOcrNet/)). Whether it can load a custom or arabic recognizer plus dictionary through its API was not confirmed.

## Windows.Media.Ocr is only an English fast path

Windows.Media.Ocr needs no bundled model but depends on OCR language packs the user installs via Settings or `Add-WindowsCapability`, which download from Windows Update ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/powertoys/text-extractor)). With no fa-IR pack on the observed 35-language list (ar-SA present), it cannot be the Persian engine; whether the Arabic pack reads Persian text is untested. It could serve as an English-only quick path, though that adds a second code path and a user-installed dependency for little gain over Tesseract `eng`. The newer Windows App SDK `TextRecognizer` runs only on NPU devices and lists no languages, so it is unsuitable for a general utility ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/ai/apis/text-recognition)). The absence claim rests on user-posted output from one machine, and no complete Microsoft language list was found.

## Recommendation, the bake-off, and Windows platform notes

**Recommendation.** Build the utility around a small engine interface, ship Tesseract 5 (`TesseractOCR` wrapper, tessdata_fast `fas`+`eng`, `--oem 1`) as the default, and run a bake-off against RapidOcrNet with the arabic model before committing. Tesseract wins on Persian specificity, model size and dependency simplicity. Choose RapidOCR instead only if the bake-off shows it clearly better on your screenshots, because it costs you a right-to-left line-grouping step and ships a model whose Persian coverage is indirect. If both fall short on Persian digits or small text, the upgrade path is to export ParisaOCR to ONNX (plausible but unverified; check Kraken's exporter) or fine-tune the Paddle recognizer and convert with Paddle2Onnx. Starting settings from general knowledge: upscale small crops 2-3x to an x-height of about 20-30 px, convert to grayscale, invert light-on-dark, add a border of about 10 px, and use `--psm 6` for blocks, 7 for single lines and 11 for sparse labels, following Tesseract's guidance of roughly 300 DPI effective resolution ([ImproveQuality](https://github.com/tesseract-ocr/tesseract/wiki/ImproveQuality/5bc360ad90a69dd2c924066e7a50388b785f29b7)). Create the engine once and reuse it.

**Uncertainties to test empirically.** Build a labelled set of real captures (Windows UI, browsers, PDFs, chat apps; sizes 10 to 24 px; light and dark themes; 100% to 200% scaling) and measure the following. First, character and word error for Tesseract fast versus best `fas`, `fas+eng` versus separate runs, and RapidOCR arabic, since no screen-text benchmark exists for any engine. Second, Persian digits ۰-۹, Arabic-Indic digits and ASCII digits, including digits inside Persian sentences. Third, ZWNJ preservation and lam-alef ligatures. Fourth, ordering of mixed Persian-English lines and CTC dropout of Latin or digit runs. Fifth, whether `fas` includes adequate Latin glyphs and whether `fas+eng` confuses scripts. Sixth, cold-start and warm latency, peak RAM and CPU on a modest laptop, since all such figures here are estimates. Seventh, exact file sizes (tessdata `fas`/`eng`/best, native DLLs, ONNX Runtime, SkiaSharp, total publish size). Eighth, `TesseractOCR` behaviour with `fas+eng` and under single-file publish. Ninth, whether the Arabic Windows pack reads Persian, as a curiosity only. Finally, verify the Persian dictionary contents for the arabic Paddle model, the model licensing terms of any Bina model, and the ParisaOCR ONNX conversion.

**Windows platform notes.** The notes' sourced facts here are narrow; the rest is general knowledge to verify. Single-file publishing supports ReadyToRun, `IncludeNativeLibrariesForSelfExtract` to bundle native libraries and `EnableCompressionInSingleFile` at a startup cost, with extraction to `%TEMP%/.net` ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/core/deploying/single-file/overview)); single-file output is OS and architecture specific. WPF apps published with `PublishTrimmed` have known failures (issue titles only were seen), so avoid trimming ([dotnet/wpf mirror](https://lightrun.com/answers/dotnet-wpf-published-selfcontained-wpf-app-with-publishtrimmed-option-set-to-true-fails-with-unhandled-exceptio)). Unverified estimates: a self-contained WPF .NET 8 single file is about 60-70 MB uncompressed and 35-45 MB compressed, while framework-dependent builds are a few MB but require the .NET 8 Desktop Runtime. Ship vcruntime140 and msvcp140 app-local if the native OCR DLLs need them, and expect only x64 (no official ARM64 Tesseract build; see charlesw #682). Tray options are `Hardcodet.NotifyIcon.Wpf`, the maintained `H.NotifyIcon.WPF` ([README](https://github.com/dotMorten/H.NotifyIcon.WPF/blob/master/README.md)) or WinForms `NotifyIcon`. For the hotkey, P/Invoke `RegisterHotKey` through an `HwndSource` hook with `MOD_NOREPEAT`, handle registration failure, and avoid combinations owned by the shell such as PrtScn and Win+Shift+S. For DPI, declare PerMonitorV2 in the manifest, capture in physical pixels across the virtual screen with `Graphics.CopyFromScreen`, and size the overlay in pixels with `SetWindowPos` (or one overlay per monitor) to avoid WPF device-independent-pixel conversion errors on mixed-DPI setups. Display recognized text in a WPF control from logical-order strings and let the platform's bidi handle presentation. None of these API behaviours was confirmed against Microsoft documentation in the research, so each needs a quick prototype.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the question: "lightest" is not the hard part, since every engine's models are under about 20 MB and the .NET runtime dwarfs them. The decisive variable is Persian fidelity on small screen text, where neither of the established engines has evidence and where a 2026 generation of tiny Persian-specific recognizers outperforms Tesseract on printed books by the authors' own measurements. That suggests designing the app so the recognizer is swappable, and treating Tesseract `fas` as the zero-cost baseline rather than the destination.

The most valuable next step is a one-day measurement, not more reading: capture 100 to 200 real Persian and mixed regions, score all candidates for character error, digits and ZWNJ, and check whether a ParisaOCR ONNX export is feasible. If that export works and its screen-text accuracy holds, a detector-free line-recognizer path (projection-profile line splitting on a user-selected region) could beat both mainstream engines at about 12 MB.
